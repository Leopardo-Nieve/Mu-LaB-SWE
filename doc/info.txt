I realised I missed a much simpler way to apply the centred scheme. The only value I actually need at the midpoint between two nodes is the depth. I found out they simply used the average depth. 
Then, I can use a finite difference scheme with two neighbouring nodes to find the 2nd order centred gradient. No need for another array with complex indices that cost a bunch of memory. I just need to recalculate that at each timestep, which is why it might be quicker to divide the bed slope and hydrostatic pressure gradients, since the bed slope is steady. 

To apply this, maybe I have to remove the dx/2 offset of cells at the boundary. Otherwise, calculating the force towards towads a boundary from a boundary cell is more complex.

After having read Zhou's treatment of boundary conditions and Guo's explanation of the 4 different bounceback schemes, I determined that using the "modified bounceback scheme" could work. 
I apply the bounce back on the boundary nodes BEFORE the streaming and collision steps. This allows the fullway bounceback to become second order (one time step to "feel" the boundary instead of 2)
